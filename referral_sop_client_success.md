## Rules of Engagement

These are the behaviors that protect the quality of the system. Violating them doesn't just hurt the referral rate — it erodes the relationships that make this work at all.

### 01 — Never ask before the moment

Asking for a referral before a client has a win to talk about feels transactional. Wait for the trigger. If there's no clear trigger, it's not time.

### 02 — One person, not a list

The ask is for one specific person the client is already thinking about. "Anyone you know" is a question. "Is there one person" is an invitation. The specificity lowers resistance.

### 03 — Mark does the ask — not CS team

The CS team briefs, prepares, and supports. But the ask itself comes from Mark. The relationship is what makes this work. A form or email from operations does not carry the same weight.

### 04 — Don't lead with the bonus

Accredited investors with real networks don't refer for $2,500. They refer because it reflects well on them. Mention the referral incentive after they've given a name — never as the opening frame.

### 05 — The story drop is not a pitch

No sales language, no urgency, no CTA beyond "forward this." The moment it sounds like marketing, pilots delete it. It should sound like a text Mark sent to a friend.

### 06 — Close every loop

Every client who gives a name gets an update on what happened. Every successful referral gets a personal thank-you from Mark. Silence after a referral is a relationship failure.

### 07 — If a client says no, that's fine

Never follow up more than once on a declined ask. Log it, note the reason if given, move on. Pressure destroys the relationship this whole system depends on.

## Referral Trigger Map

These are the specific moments in a client's journey where a referral ask is most likely to land. The CS team is responsible for detecting these in real time and flagging them for activation within 48 hours.

| Trigger Moment | What It Looks Like | Track |
|---|---|---|
| First deal closed | Client reports completing their first land transaction — any size | Coaching |
| First check deposited | Client confirms first payment received on a deal — cash or installment | Coaching |
| Portfolio milestone hit | Client crosses $50K, $100K, or $250K invested or returned | Coaching |
| Strong session win | Coaching call ends with client expressing high energy, gratitude, or breakthrough | Coaching |
| Unsolicited praise | Client sends a positive email, DM, or comment unprompted | Both |
| 90-day check-in win | Quarterly review shows strong progress relative to stated goals | Coaching |
| Flight school graduation | Student completes final module and posts in community or emails success | Flight School |
| Community brag post | Student shares a deal or win in the community forum or Facebook group | Flight School |
| One-year anniversary | Client has been enrolled for 12 months — good moment for relationship reflection | Both |

### Critical Rule: 48-Hour Activation Window

When a trigger fires, the CS team must flag it and brief Mark within 48 hours. The emotional peak fades fast. A week later, the moment is gone. Log the trigger in CRM immediately with a note: **REFERRAL TRIGGER — ACTION NEEDED.**

## Track 1 — Coaching Clients

### The Peak Moment Ask

This is a one-to-one, personal ask from Mark — not a program, not a form, not a broadcast. The ask is for one person. The bar is low by design. The CS team's job is to identify the moment and put Mark in position to execute it.

### Step 1: CS team builds the Peak Moment List

Weekly review of coaching notes, CRM activity, and check-in call summaries. Flag any client who hit a meaningful milestone in the last 7 days. Add to the Peak Moment List in CRM with milestone type, date, and a one-sentence context note. Target: 4–6 names on this list at any given time.

> **Owner:** CS Team · **Cadence:** Weekly, Monday

### Step 2: CS team briefs Mark with a 3-line context card

Before Mark reaches out, send him a brief note with exactly three pieces of information: (1) what the client achieved, (2) how long they've been working with us, (3) any relevant personal detail (profession, location, network type). Keep it under 5 sentences. This is prep, not a report.

**Example brief to Mark:**

> *[Client Name] just closed her third deal this quarter — $28K total returned. She's been in the program 14 months. She's a dentist with a practice partner who's mentioned wanting to diversify. Good moment for the ask.*

> **Owner:** CS Team · **Trigger:** Within 48hrs of milestone

### Step 3: Mark executes the one-person ask

Mark reaches out personally — text, voice note, or short call. Not email. The ask is for one name, not a referral. The framing matters: it's about the prospect, not the program. See the Scripts section for the exact language.

> **Owner:** Mark · **Timing:** Within 72hrs of brief

### Step 4: CS team sets up the warm intro flow

Once Mark gets a name, CS team takes over logistics: schedule the three-way intro call, send the referrer a simple confirmation with what to expect, and prep Mark with the prospect's background. Use the Intro Support Template in the Scripts section.

> **Owner:** CS Team · **Timing:** Within 24hrs of receiving a name

### Step 5: Log, close the loop, and recognize

Update CRM with referral status. Once a referred prospect enrolls, CS team prompts Mark to send a personal thank-you (text or handwritten note). Log the referring client as a Tier 1 or Tier 2 advocate in the referral tier system.

> **Owner:** CS Team + Mark · **Timing:** Within 48hrs of enrollment

### Execution Note: Who to Prioritize First

Start the list by looking backwards 90 days. Pull every coaching client who hit a milestone in that window. These are warm re-activations — the moment is less fresh but the relationship is strong. This gives the team immediate traction while the live trigger system is being built.

- Pull all clients with a deal closed in last 90 days
- Pull all clients who sent unsolicited positive feedback in last 60 days
- Rank by network quality (business owners and real estate investors first)
- Brief Mark with top 5 names to start

## Track 2 — Flight School Clients

### The Peer Story Drop

Pilots forward things to pilots. This track doesn't ask for a referral directly — it gives flight school clients a compelling peer story and a low-friction way to share it. The content does the work. The CS team handles production and distribution.

### Step 1: CS team identifies the story subject

Look for a flight school graduate or current student with a tangible win — a deal closed, a portfolio built, a lifestyle outcome. Prioritize: pilots, high-income professionals, or anyone with a vivid before/after story. Get their permission and a few quotes or details.

> **Owner:** CS Team · **Cadence:** Monthly

### Step 2: Mark records a short, informal story drop

2–4 minute video or voice note from Mark — not polished, not produced. The format is: "I wanted you to see what [first name] did with this." Specific numbers, specific timeline, honest reflection. This is not marketing copy. It sounds like Mark talking to a friend.

**Story drop structure (Mark follows this loosely):**

> Who they are → what they were skeptical about → first deal → what surprised them → where they are now → "if you know someone in your flying circle who'd appreciate hearing this, forward it — that's it."

> **Owner:** Mark · **Duration:** 2–4 min · **Format:** Video or voice note

### Step 3: CS team formats and sends the drop

Package the story as a simple email to the flight school client list. Subject line includes the student's first name and a specific outcome (not a tease). Keep the email body short — 4 sentences max, then the content. No pitch. No CTA beyond "forward it if it's useful." Include a single link to a landing page with a soft lead capture.

> **Owner:** CS Team · **Timing:** Within 5 days of recording

### Step 4: Track forwards, replies, and new inquiries

Monitor email open rate, forward rate, and any new inbound leads from the landing page link. Any reply to the story drop email gets a personal response from CS team within 24 hours. Any lead coming through the link gets added to the pipeline with "flight school referral" source tag.

> **Owner:** CS Team · **Window:** 14 days post-send

### Step 5: Recognize the story subject

The student whose story was featured gets a personal note from Mark and a community spotlight. This reinforces the behavior and creates a virtuous cycle — other students see what happens when they share wins publicly.

> **Owner:** CS Team prompts, Mark sends · **Timing:** Same week

### Execution Note: The Flight School Community Angle

Pilots are a tight, trust-heavy tribe. The shared identity matters more than the investment. When the story drop explicitly references flying — a pilot's schedule flexibility, time freedom, the parallels between systems thinking in cockpits and land investing — it resonates differently than generic real estate copy. Make sure Mark weaves this in naturally when recording.

## Scripts and Templates

*Exact language for each touch. These are starting points — Mark should adjust tone to match each relationship. The structure is what matters. Never lead with the program. Always lead with the client's achievement or the prospect's potential gain.*

### The Peak Moment Ask (text or voice note)

**Mark to coaching client — after milestone**

> "Hey [Name] — I've been watching what you've built over the last [timeframe] and honestly it's impressive. I want to ask you something. Is there one person in your world — just one — who you think should know what you know now? Not a pitch, not a presentation. Just a conversation. I'll handle everything after that. Anyone come to mind?"

**Key rules:** One person, not "anyone you know." Framed as a favor to the prospect, not a favor to Mark. No mention of bonus or incentive in first ask.

### Brief to Mark (internal, before the ask)

**CS Team → Mark Slack/email brief**

> "[Client Name] just [specific milestone]. They've been with us [duration]. They're a [profession] based in [location] — their network likely includes [relevant peer type]. Good window for the peak moment ask. Want me to schedule a 5-min check-in call as the vehicle, or will you text them directly?"

### Intro Support Message (after name is given)

**CS Team → Referrer, once they've given a name**

> "That's great — thank you for thinking of [Prospect Name]. Here's what happens next: Mark will reach out to you first so you can make the intro however feels most natural — text, email, or we can set up a short three-way call. You don't need to sell anything. Just say you think this person would appreciate a conversation. We'll take it from there. Does Tuesday or Thursday work for a quick call to set this up?"

### Story Drop Email (to flight school list)

**Email to flight school client list**

> **Subject:** What [First Name] did in 8 months
>
> I wanted to share something with you — not a pitch, just a story.
>
> [First Name] finished flight school [timeframe] ago. He was skeptical about whether this would actually work around his schedule. Here's what happened: [2-sentence specific outcome].
>
> I recorded a short video about his journey — [link].
>
> If you know someone in your flying circle who'd appreciate hearing this, forward it. That's it.

### Personal Thank-You (after referral enrolls)

**Mark → Referrer, after new student enrolls**

> "Hey [Name] — I wanted you to know that [Prospect] just joined. That intro you made mattered. I don't take that lightly. Thank you."

*Short on purpose. No bonus mention in this message. The recognition is the point. If there's a referral incentive, CS handles that separately.*

## Tracking and Cadence

*What to log and when to review. Every referral touch gets logged. The CS team owns the CRM hygiene. Mark does not track this — he just does the ask. The CS team surfaces patterns and optimization flags monthly.*

### CRM Fields to Log

Required fields for every referral activity:

- Client name + tier (Coaching or Flight School)
- Trigger type and trigger date
- Date Mark was briefed
- Date ask was executed
- Prospect name given (if any) + referral source tag
- Intro call scheduled (Y/N) + date
- Enrollment outcome + date
- Thank-you sent by Mark (Y/N)

### Weekly Review (CS Team)

- Review coaching notes for milestone triggers
- Update Peak Moment List in CRM
- Brief Mark on any new names
- Follow up on any open intro calls
- Check flight school community for brag posts / graduation

### Monthly Review (CS Team → Mark)

- Publish story drop to flight school list
- Run conversion rate report (referrals → enrollments)
- Review which trigger types are converting best
- Flag any dormant advocates for re-engagement
- Present 1-page summary to Mark

### Optimization Signals

*Flags to surface in monthly review:*

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Conversion rate below 10% | Review ask language and trigger timing |
| Zero names given from asks | Brief Mark on objection handling |
| Flight school forwards below 5% | Review story subject quality |
| Same clients being triggered repeatedly | Expand the eligible pool |
| More than 7 days between trigger and ask | Fix the 48-hour activation rule |
